AH-64D Symbology Guide

The four IHADSS modes as they look in DCS, with every element numbered on a real screenshot. Click a marker or a legend line to pin it. The selection stays highlighted, and the pinned name and description stay at the bottom of the screen while you scroll.

Which mode am I in?

The game does not show a mode name anywhere. You identify the mode from what is on the display. Work through these in order.

  1. 1. Is there a solid horizon line?Yes: Cruise. It also has a pitch ladder and an MSL altitude number.
  2. 2. Is the horizon line dashed?Yes: Transition. A velocity vector line sits on the centre cross.
  3. 3. No horizon line at all?You are in Hover or Bob-Up.
  4. 4. Is there an octagon on the ground?Yes: Bob-Up. No octagon: Hover.

Switch: on the cyclic, push the Symbology Select Switch forward ("CT") to toggle Cruise and Transition. Push it aft ("HB") to toggle Hover and Bob-Up. Pressing it in brings up the FLT page on the MPD. The switch toggles within a pair, so one press from Transition does not reach Hover. Press AFT first.

Cruise

Symbology Select Switch FWD ("CT")

How to recognise it

Solid horizon line, pitch ladder and a barometric altitude number on the right.

When to use it

Instrument flying and checking MSL altitude. Transition does not show MSL altitude.

  1. Ownship heading tapeScale across the top. The number in the middle is your current heading. It slides as you turn.
  2. Command heading caretUp-arrow under the tape showing the heading to steer toward your selected waypoint. Turn until it sits under the tape centre.
  3. Alternate sensor bearingSmall caret marking where the other crewmember's sensor is pointing, shown against the heading tape.
  4. Flight path vector (FPV)Circle with a stub. Shows where the aircraft is actually going, not where the nose points. Place it on your landing or obstacle point.
  5. Head trackerDiamond chevrons that follow your head. They mark where your helmet is looking relative to the symbology.
  6. Helmet line-of-sight reticle and cueing dotsCross fixed in the monocle. Your aiming point and a fixed pitch reference. Dots beside it show which direction the selected target or waypoint lies (see the Cueing section).
  7. Cued line-of-sight reticleDashed plus showing where the acquisition source (waypoint or TADS target) is, when it is inside your HDU field of view.
  8. Pitch ladderHorizontal bars for 10 degree pitch steps. Dashed bars are below the horizon. Cruise mode only.
  9. Engine torque (%)Torque percentage. Watch it on climbs, hovers and when pulling collective.
  10. Barometric altitude (ft)Altitude above mean sea level. Only Cruise shows this.
  11. Navigation fly-to cuePentagon waypoint symbol. Fly toward it to track to the selected waypoint.
  12. Radar altimeter (ft)Height above the ground directly below you.
  13. Vertical speed scale (ft/min)Tick scale with a pointer. The pointer shows climb or descent rate.
  14. True airspeed (kts)Speed through the air in knots.
  15. Cruise horizon lineSolid line. It stays level with the real horizon and tilts as you bank. A solid line means Cruise.
  16. Trim ballSlip indicator. Keep it centred with pedals.
  17. Waypoint data blockSelected waypoint (W01), ground speed, distance to waypoint (5.4 KM) and time to waypoint (3:28).
  18. Sight selectionWhich sight is driving your view. P-HMD is Pilot Helmet Sight.
  19. RangeRange to the current acquisition source, in km.
  20. High action display (FOV / FOR box)Box showing where your sensor field of view sits inside its field of regard. Full detail in the Cueing section.
  21. Acquisition sourceSource the sensors are cued to. Here TADS.

Transition

Symbology Select Switch FWD ("CT"), pressed again

How to recognise it

Dashed horizon line, a velocity vector line from the centre cross, and no pitch ladder or MSL altitude.

When to use it

Most phases of flight except takeoff, landing and hover.

  1. Ownship heading tapeCurrent heading in the centre of the tape.
  2. Cued line-of-sight reticleDashed plus. Shows where your acquisition source is when it is in the HDU field of view.
  3. Flight path vector (FPV)Where the aircraft is actually going. Keep it slightly above the landing point on an approach.
  4. Navigation fly-to cueWaypoint symbol to steer toward.
  5. Head trackerChevrons that follow your head.
  6. Acceleration cueCircle at the end of the line. Forward cyclic pushes it up the line, aft cyclic pulls it back. Keep it behind the line tip to decelerate smoothly.
  7. Velocity vectorLine from the centre cross. Its length is ground speed. The upper tip is the 40 kt position here.
  8. Helmet line-of-sight reticle and cueing dotsCross fixed in the monocle. The velocity vector starts here.
  9. Transition horizon lineDashed horizon. Dashes mean Transition. Level it with the real horizon on takeoff.
  10. Engine torque (%)Torque percentage.
  11. True airspeed (kts)Speed through the air.
  12. Radar altimeter (ft)Height above ground. Replaces the MSL reading of Cruise.
  13. Vertical speed pointerTriangle marking climb or descent rate on the scale.
  14. Radar altimeter tapeBar scale beside the number. Gives a quick visual on low altitude.
  15. Trim ballSlip indicator. Centre with pedals.
  16. High action displaySensor field of view and field of regard box, with sight, range and acquisition source labels.

Hover

Symbology Select Switch AFT ("HB")

How to recognise it

No horizon line. A long line from the centre cross ending in a circle. A radar altimeter tape on the right.

When to use it

Taxi, takeoff, landing and any hover.

  1. Ownship heading tapeCurrent heading in the centre of the tape.
  2. Command heading caretHeading to steer toward your waypoint.
  3. Head trackerChevrons that follow your head.
  4. Cued line-of-sight reticleDashed plus showing the acquisition source direction when it is in view.
  5. Engine torque (%)Torque percentage.
  6. Acceleration cueCircle at the end of the line. Cyclic input moves it before the aircraft starts to drift. Centre it on the cross to stop.
  7. Velocity vectorLine from the centre cross. Top-down view of your drift. Length is ground speed and saturates at 6 kts.
  8. Helmet line-of-sight reticle and cueing dotsCross at roughly the position of the rotor mast. The velocity vector starts here.
  9. True airspeed (kts)In a hover this reads small numbers (4 here).
  10. Radar altimeter (ft)Height above ground, 5 ft here. Your most important number in a hover.
  11. Vertical speed pointerTriangle marking climb or descent rate on the scale.
  12. Radar altimeter tapeScale beside the number showing low altitude at a glance.
  13. High action displaySensor field of view and field of regard box, plus sight and acquisition source labels.

Bob-Up

Symbology Select Switch AFT ("HB"), pressed again

How to recognise it

Hover symbology plus an octagon on the ground ahead.

When to use it

Settling into a precise hover over a spot, such as a hover check or a landing point.

  1. Ownship heading tapeCurrent heading in the centre of the tape.
  2. Command heading caretHeading to steer toward your waypoint.
  3. Cued line-of-sight reticleDashed plus showing the acquisition source direction when it is in view.
  4. Engine torque (%)Torque percentage.
  5. Head trackerChevrons that follow your head.
  6. Acceleration cueCircle at the end of the line. Same use as in Hover.
  7. Velocity vectorLine from the centre cross showing drift direction and speed. Saturates at 6 kts.
  8. Helmet line-of-sight reticle and cueing dotsYour position. Compare it with the Bob-Up box to see how far you have moved.
  9. Bob-Up box12 sq ft box anchored where you were when you entered the mode. It stays until you change mode. At the edge of the display you have moved 40 ft.
  10. True airspeed (kts)Speed through the air.
  11. Radar altimeter (ft)Height above ground.
  12. Vertical speed pointerTriangle marking climb or descent rate on the scale.
  13. Radar altimeter tapeScale beside the number.

Cueing and High Action Display

Shown in every mode

Cueing dots

A dot to the left of your reticle plus a dot above it means the target is up and to the left. No dot means it is in line on that axis.

Field of regard

PNVS: +20 / -45 degrees elevation, 90 degrees left or right. TADS: +30 / -60 degrees elevation, 120 degrees left or right.

  1. Helmet line-of-sight reticleThe fixed cross. The cueing dots sit beside it. It flashes when the line of sight is invalid, or when the NVS switch is NORM and the sensors are at their slew limits.
  2. Cued line-of-sight reticleDashed plus at the position of the acquisition source. Only drawn when the source is inside your HDU field of view.
  3. Trim ballSlip indicator that rides on top of the High Action Display. Keep it centred with pedals.
  4. Cued line-of-sight dotDot inside the field of regard box. Shows the relative azimuth and elevation of the acquisition source within the field of regard.
  5. Field-of-view boxSmall rectangle. The 30 x 40 degree PNVS or TADS field of view, positioned inside the field of regard.
  6. Field-of-regard boxLarge box. The azimuth and elevation limits of the selected sensor. Tick marks along the edges mark the limits.
  7. Sight selection and rangeSelected sight (P-HMD) and range value.
  8. Acquisition sourceSource the sensors are cued to. TADS here.
